Chapter 1. The opportunity

Same car, different price

Vehicle prices vary significantly across European markets. Italy had a surplus of EVs while Denmark had high demand. Dealers could profit by selling stock across the border to other dealers, not just to consumers.

Working with autoproff, alongside a PM, engineers, a researcher and content design, we researched how dealers sold B2B today and where the friction sat.

Chapter 2. What I designed

Publish once, sell across borders

I designed a flow that lets dealers publish their existing AutoScout24 listings straight to the B2B marketplace.

Chapter 3. The decision

Opt in or opt out

What research said

Dealers wanted to opt in, not be defaulted in.

The decision went the other way. It launched opt-out.

How do dealers join?
Research recommendation

Opt in. Dealers choose to take part.

Adopted later
What launched

Opt out. Dealers are added by default.

Chapter 4. Results

Supply worked. Demand didn't.

Listings and dealers arrived fast. But dealers had been defaulted in rather than choosing to take part. Offers arrived and went unanswered.

A marketplace full of listings, with dealers who don't reply, isn't a marketplace. It's a catalogue.

Chapter 5. What happened next

Research, vindicated. Mostly.

The approach has since moved to opt-in. Inactive dealers were cleared out at the same time, and seller response jumped in the week that followed. I'd moved into a new role by then.

Being honest about it

  • The cleanup and the default change landed together, so neither can be isolated.
  • Offer volume fell sharply as the dealer base shrank.
  • Response improved. Sales did not follow.

Opt-in fixed the problem the research predicted, and revealed another underneath. A responsive dealer isn't automatically a buying one.
