Selling Cars Across Europe

A real market opportunity, a test of scale, and what it taught us about demand.

Chapter 1. Context

Same car, different price

Car prices vary a lot across Europe. For example, Italy had a surplus of EVs while Denmark couldn't get enough, so dealers could profit by selling stock across the border to other dealers. Unlocking Europe-wide trade between dealers was a big opportunity, if selling across the border could be as easy as listing a car.

I led design for a team of a PM, engineers, a researcher and a content designer.

Chapter 2. Problem

Why dealers held back

Selling internationally to unknown dealers felt risky. What if the buyer doesn't pay, or disputes the car once it arrives? And cross-border transport and handling can be expensive.

Research showed how B2B selling differs: cars are priced differently, damage is shown more honestly, and listings carry fewer images. Even so, dealers were open to anything that could increase their margin.

Chapter 3. What I did

Publish once, sell across borders

AutoProff, our B2B auction platform, already solved the risk and the cost, with escrow payments and scaled pricing for transport and handling. So the value we could add was getting listings onto AutoProff with the least possible friction.

I designed a flow that publishes a dealer's existing AutoScout24 listings straight to AutoProff, so there's no second marketplace to manage. To fit how B2B works, we cut the number of mandatory images and added a dedicated B2B price field and an optional B2B description.

The hypothesis

A marketplace can't prove demand without supply. Could an opt-out launch, listing dealers' stock by default, reach the scale we need to validate the supply and demand imbalance and unlock margin across Europe?

Decision

Use the consumer price, and say so.

Combined with opt-out, the B2B price field became a blocker: we couldn't reach scale if every dealer had to enter a second price. So we let listings use their B2C price, and signalled it clearly to buyers on AutoProff so they could negotiate accurately.

Chapter 4. Results

Supply worked. Demand didn't.

Scale came quickly: thousands of listings from dealers across Italy and Germany. But many of those dealers hadn't set out to sell to other dealers, and offers went unanswered.

A marketplace full of listings, with dealers who don't reply, isn't a marketplace. It's a catalogue.

Chapter 5. Next steps

From scale to engagement.

With the supply side proven, the platform moved to opt-in and cleared out inactive dealers, focusing on those who wanted to trade. Seller response jumped the following week. I'd moved to a new role by then.

Because both changes landed together, neither can be isolated. Offer volume fell as the dealer base shrank, and while response improved, sales didn't follow.

That revealed the next problem to solve: a responsive dealer isn't automatically a buying one.
