Chapter 1. Context

One inventory, all of Europe

How could we use the scale of AutoScout24's Europe-wide inventory to supercharge our dealer-to-dealer offering, while helping dealers win along the way? I led design for a team of a PM, engineers, a researcher and a content designer to find out.

Chapter 2. The problem

Why dealers held back

With our UX researcher, I designed the research questions and interview guide, and conducted several of the interviews in Italy myself. We wanted to understand why dealers weren't using AutoProff, our dealer-to-dealer marketplace, for the same inventory they published on AutoScout24.

The findings were clear. B2B listings work differently, with their own pricing, more honest damage reporting and fewer images. They were also often less profitable than selling to consumers.

Chapter 3. Design

Publish once, sell across borders

I then ran a workshop to turn our findings into a prioritised list of opportunities. Adding B2B-specific information to listings was easy to solve with little effort. And we knew that opening dealers' local inventory to a European audience through AutoProff could increase their profit margins, by making use of differences in demand across markets.

I designed a flow that publishes a dealer's existing AutoScout24 listings straight to AutoProff, so there's no second marketplace to manage. To fit how B2B works, we cut the number of mandatory images and added a dedicated B2B price field and an optional B2B description.

Chapter 4. Impact

Supply grew. Conversion stalled.

Supply grew quickly, with thousands of listings added to the marketplace from Germany and Italy. And while demand followed at a healthy rate, we hadn't accounted for dealers ignoring leads, which caused a conversion problem on the AutoProff platform.

Chapter 5. What next

From scale to engagement.

With the supply side proven, the platform moved to opt-in and cleared out inactive dealers, focusing on those who wanted to trade. Seller response jumped the following week.
