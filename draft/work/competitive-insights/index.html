Dealer Pricing Insights

Turning a manual research chore into an in-context pricing signal.

Lead designer

Chapter 1. Context

Pricing by eye

To price a car, dealers searched consumer marketplaces by hand, weekly and sometimes daily, eyeballing equipment and condition before making a gut call.

I led design for a team of a PM, engineers, a UX researcher and a content designer, to replace that with a real pricing signal.

Chapter 2. Problem

The wrong competitors

With the research team, I ran new interviews and reviewed existing research. Dealers checked the competition when sourcing stock, creating a listing and in regular reviews, to stay competitive or to work out why a car wasn't selling. It broke down in the same places: comparing equipment was slow, and they couldn't see how long rival cars had been standing or how they performed.

The deeper finding: from the centre of the marketplace, we could see that dealers' idea of their competition often didn't match the cars buyers actually compared.

Chapter 3. What I did

Real competition, right where they price

Workshops with the team turned those insights into two decisions.

Decision 1

Compare against what buyers cross-shop.

Rather than let dealers pick their rivals, we used the cars buyers actually viewed side by side. That also let us reuse our existing recommender instead of building a new model, so we got to market much faster.

Decision 2

Put it inside the listing.

The first prototype was a separate "Compare your vehicle" page. Testing showed pricing against the competition is part of dealers' core workflow, not a side task, so we moved it into the listing, next to its own performance and price.

Chapter 4. Results

The flagship of a new tier

Competitive Insights became the flagship feature for moving dealers onto our second price tier, and within six months the most-used feature in the DealerIQ suite in Germany and Italy. Dealers on our Dealer Council said it helped them justify prices and adjust their strategy.

Chapter 5. Next steps

What v2 needs

Reusing the recommender got us to market fast, but its matching was too broad for pricing: diesel next to electric, automatic next to manual, and ranking less precise than dealers expected.

For v2, dealers asked for sold-car data, competitor sets they can customise, and proof that a price change leads to a sale.
