Dealer Pricing Insights

Turning a manual research chore into an in-context pricing signal.

Chapter 1. The problem

Pricing by eye

I was the designer on a cross-functional product team, working with a PM, engineers, a UX researcher and a content designer.

To price competitively, dealers searched consumer marketplaces by hand. Weekly, sometimes daily. They eyeballed differences in equipment and condition across listings, then made a gut-feel call on price.

Chapter 2. Process

Research, workshops, dealers

I combined new primary research, run with the research team, with a review of existing work.

I ran ideation workshops with the product team, then built prototypes and tested them directly with dealers.

Chapter 3. The key decision

Whose competitors?

Key decision

Build competitor sets from what buyers actually cross-shopped, not from who dealers thought their competitors were.

Research showed dealers' own view of their competition was often flawed or incomplete. Comparing against real buyer behaviour was something dealers had no way to do themselves.

Two ways to define a competitor

Who dealers think they compete withAssumed, from experience
Who buyers actually cross-shopFrom real search behaviour

Illustrative. The assumed set often missed the cars buyers were really comparing.

Chapter 4. Impact

The most-used feature in the suite

Within six months it was the most-used feature in the DealerIQ suite in Germany and Italy.

Dealers on our Dealer Council said it helped them justify pricing decisions and adjust their strategy.

Chapter 5. What I'd do next

What v2 needs

Default competitor matching was too broad, mixing diesel with electric and automatic with manual. Price ranking didn't yet meet dealers' expectations for precision.

Dealers were explicit about v2: transaction and sold data, customisable competitor sets, and evidence that a price change turns into a sale.
